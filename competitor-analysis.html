🦁 Crowdfunding Competitor Analysis

Fantasy, Sci-Fi & Indie Publishing Campaigns (2022–2026) · Compiled February 2026

Executive Summary

  • Publishing on Kickstarter grew 21.7% between 2022–2024, partly fueled by Sanderson
  • BackerKit emerged as a viable alternative — Sanderson's Words of Radiance became its most-funded project ever
  • Conservative/independent publishers (Rippaverse, Ark Press) proved massive underserved demand
  • Average pledge amounts: $40–$290 for successful book campaigns
  • 2024 was Kickstarter's biggest year: $706M raised across all categories
  • Email list size is the #1 predictor of campaign success

Mega-Campaigns ($10M+)

1. Brandon Sanderson — "Surprise! Four Secret Novels" (2022)

Platform: Kickstarter Raised: $41,754,153 Backers: 185,341 Avg Pledge: ~$225

Tiers: $40 (4 ebooks) → $160 (4 hardcovers) → $360 (ebooks + 8 swag boxes) → $500 (all formats + swag)

What worked: Surprise reveal format → massive FOMO. "Year of Sanderson" subscription model increased avg pledge. Campaign page written as a personal letter — authentic tone. Cross-media coverage (NYT, CNBC, The Verge) drove non-fan traffic.

Lesson: Build your audience for years BEFORE crowdfunding. The campaign harvests; it doesn't create.

2. Brandon Sanderson — "Words of Radiance" Leatherbound (2024)

Platform: BackerKit Raised: $23M+ Backers: 55,000+ Avg Pledge: ~$290–$420

What worked: First major creator on BackerKit at this scale — proved the platform works. "Last 10 minutes reset timer" feature added ~$1M in bonus revenue. Surprise secret project mid-campaign boosted pledges.

Lesson: Premium physical products (leatherbound, special editions) command much higher margins than ebooks. Mid-campaign reveals keep momentum.

3. Cosmere® RPG — Brotherwise Games (2024)

Platform: Kickstarter Raised: $14,556,997 Backers: 52,648 Avg Pledge: ~$277

Lesson: Your book IP is licensable. Tabletop, games, merchandise are all crowdfundable extensions. Most funded Games campaign in KS history.

Rippaverse Lessons for Lion

  • Self-hosted store saved ~$200K–$400K in platform fees per campaign
  • Eric July's personal ~1M YouTube following was the primary marketing channel
  • Fans felt they were "supporting a movement" — powerful but unsustainable alone
  • Higher avg purchase per remaining buyer suggests core fans spending more, but casual audience evaporated
  • For Lion: The storytelling-first angle is smarter than leading with ideology. Build genuine readers, not just political supporters.

Mid-Tier & Indie Campaigns ($100K–$1M)

Mongoose Publishing — Traveller RPG (2024–2025)

Platform: Kickstarter Range: £100K–£170K per campaign Backers: ~1,000–1,200 (stable) Avg Pledge: £100–£165

Lesson: Niche publishers can run repeated successful campaigns with a loyal ~1,000-backer core. Consistency beats spectacle. This is the most comparable model to Lion's realistic first campaign.

Larry Correia — American Paladin / Ark Press (2025)

Platform: Kickstarter Status: Pre-launch (signups active)

Major bestselling author leaving Baen Books for crowdfunding. Using "secret rewards" for email signup incentive. Also signed with Aethon Books for broader distribution (dual strategy).

Lesson: The trad-to-indie pipeline is real. Authors with 100K+ readerships can crowdfund effectively. Watch this campaign closely for comparable data.

Key Takeaways for Lion Publishing

1. Audience First, Campaign Second

Every successful campaign >$100K had a pre-existing audience. Your email list size is the #1 predictor. Everything between now and Oct 5 is audience building.

2. Tier Structure Best Practices

  • Entry ($10–$40): Ebook only — low barrier, high conversion
  • Core ($40–$100): Physical book + ebook — your "expected" tier
  • Premium ($100–$250): Special edition + extras — your margin maker
  • Superfan ($250–$500+): Everything + exclusives — small %, big revenue
  • Avoid more than 6-7 tiers; clarity beats variety

3. Stretch Goals Strategy

  • Don't publish stretch goal amounts before you fund — wait to see velocity
  • Plan 10-15 but only reveal 3-4 ahead
  • Mix cheap goals (digital content) with exciting physical goals
  • Front-load achievable goals to maintain momentum

4. The "Based" Publisher Playbook

  • Rippaverse proved the model: underserved audiences will pay premium prices
  • Key ingredients: charismatic founder + quality product + clear positioning + community-first
  • Warning: Novelty fades. 74% purchaser decline across 4 Rippaverse campaigns. Long-term success requires genuine audience growth, not just culture-war momentum.
  • Lead with storytelling quality. Let the positioning come through organically.

5. Platform Choice

  • <10K email subs: Use Kickstarter (platform discovery helps)
  • 10K–50K subs: Kickstarter or BackerKit (both work)
  • 50K+ subs: Consider self-hosted (save 5-8% in fees)

6. Fulfillment Warning

  • Budget 25-35% of revenue for printing, shipping, fulfillment
  • International shipping eats margins alive
  • Use a fulfillment partner — don't try to ship 50K packages yourself
  • Plan fulfillment waves (2-3 over 6-12 months)
