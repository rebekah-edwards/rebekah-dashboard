The Vault of Ended Kings

Destroy Algar's phylactery so he can't reform. A quiet, dread-soaked delve — mostly RP and puzzle, very little combat, and a hard scramble out as the vault collapses. The catch: the vault smothers magic, so the party's overwhelming power is, for once, off the table.

⏳ The Clock

Algar will reform in 7 days (rolled) unless the phylactery is destroyed first. No card draw No level-up — see Rewards

🗺️ The Plan (the simple version)

When the party asks "okay, how do we actually do this?", it's three steps:

  1. Find it. The phylactery hides among hundreds of identical urns. Akilles's blood makes the real one cry out — they find it by the sound.
  2. It won't break yet. The true urn is protected by a soul-anchor (the Choir) below. While the anchor is intact, the urn can't be damaged at all.
  3. Break the anchor, then the urn. Silence the twelve dead kings of the Choir (each king's name + Akilles's word of release) to destroy the anchor. Then smash the urn — Algar is gone for good.

The Layout (a simple vertical stack)

  1. The throne room (where they are) → hidden stair behind the dais.
  2. The Hall of the Ended Kings — entry hall of dead-king statues.
  3. The Gallery of Urns — the big tiered chamber where the phylactery hides.
  4. The Choir of Ended Kings — the soul-anchor chamber, directly below the Gallery.

Mood: Held-breath cold. A place that eats sound. This is where the party's power matters least and their choices matter most.

Walkthrough

1 · The Hidden Descent
The throne room is silent now. Algar is gone — when he fell, his whole body collapsed into a drift of grey dust that still hangs in the cracked, settling air. Where the throne stood, a seam of cold blue light has traced itself across the floor in the shape of a crown.
  • The walls are cracked from the fight; fine grey dust (all that's left of Algar) drifts in the light.
  • A deep cold rises from the seam.
  • Shove the throne aside, or Investigation DC 13, to open the stair.
2 · Laeren, Keeper of the Vault (RP heart)

Found in the Undercroft beneath the Dread Market — the last hereditary Keeper of the Vault, who has served the royal house his whole life.

Laeren only knows the Doomed Forgotten Realms. To him there has only ever been one Algar: the cold, lichdom-seeking King Algar he's served and feared for years. He has no concept of other universes or of a good Algar. Akilles wears the king's exact face, and Laeren can't tell them apart.

Lore reminder: Akilles is Algar — the version from the regular Realms who killed their father Aldar to stop his lichdom. This world's Algar followed Aldar into it. Same man, two universes.

Showing Akilles's face is dangerous — Laeren sees his king and reacts with terror. Two ways to run it:

  • Path A — Hide the face (recommended). Disguise Akilles (Crown of Lies / hood / keep him back) and approach as outsiders who mean to end King Algar. Worn down by years under a monster, Laeren can be persuaded to help.
  • Path B — Wear the fear. Let Laeren believe the king stands before him and lean on the terror. Darkly ironic — the Keeper helps "the king" reach the jar that ends him. Manage the questions carefully.
An old man sits with his back to a dead shrine, sharpening a knife he is far too frail to use. When strangers step in, he tenses. "...The vault's sealed. There's nothing for you. Please. I'm only the Keeper." (If Akilles's face is seen:) The knife clatters to the floor. "M-my king— I've kept it, I've kept everything, just as you— please don't—" He cannot conceive you are anyone but the man whose vault this is.

What he gives (if earned):

  • The phylactery hides among the urns; only the royal blood (Algar's own — which Akilles carries) finds it, by making the true urn cry out.
  • The urn can't break until the soul-anchor (the Choir) below is destroyed.
  • Destroy the anchor by naming and releasing the twelve dead kings — only the royal Castor blood can speak it. Laeren knows all twelve names and the words.
  • The vault smothers magic. "Go down as men, not as wizards."

Getting it out of him: Path A — roleplay (promise to end the king, mercy), spend a secret, or Persuasion DC 16. Path B — careful kingly demands. Cruelty makes him clam up.

Akilles's blood works disguised or not (the Crown changes looks, not blood). His fate is the party's: left in the Undercroft, brought to Sigil (recurring ally), or found dead later. He does not enter the vault.

3 · The Hall of the Ended Kings

Below the stair: dozens of stone effigies of kneeling kings, each holding out its own crown, every face chiseled blank.

Who are these kings? For as long as this doomed Sword Coast has existed, it's been ruled by a line of kings who each swore themselves to Vecna for power and a promise of immortality. When each died, the cult stored his death here — and those hoarded deaths let the current king cheat death and reform. The urns ahead are that long line of spent kings. Algar is just the latest.

  • The blanked faces still carry regnal marks and a carved name at each base (the names needed below — History/Investigation DC 15, or get them from Laeren).
  • An inscription repeats above them: ENDED, BUT NOT RELEASED.
4 · The Gallery of Urns — find it by sound
The hall opens into a vast round chamber, tiered like an amphitheater turned inward. On every plinth stands an urn: bonewhite, lidless, identical. Hundreds of them, climbing into dark you can't see the top of.

The true phylactery is one urn among hundreds, with no way to tell them apart by sight (even Detect Magic shows the whole room the same uniform necromancy).

  • When Akilles sheds a drop of blood, one urn begins to cry — a faint, continuous weeping sound (his own voice, if you want to twist the knife). Nothing changes visibly. They locate it by ear.
  • The crying fades if he stops bleeding — he must keep a fresh drop flowing while they cross to it (Perception to pinpoint in the echo).

For Akilles's personal haunting as he bleeds, see "The Pursuer" below.

5 · The Choir of Ended Kings (the soul-anchor)
A round vault, domed in black stone. Twelve stone sarcophagi ring the edge — the twelve greatest of the dead kings. From each, a cord of braided bone and grey sinew runs inward to a heart of black obsidian, fist-sized, floating, beating once every few seconds. With every beat the twelve sarcophagi hum a single low note together — a choir with no breath.

Plainly: the obsidian heart is the soul-anchor. While it beats, the phylactery upstairs cannot be damaged. To destroy it, release all twelve kings, one at a time.

The word of release. The vault answers to the royal Castor blood, which Akilles carries (he is Algar, same blood). Speak the king's name + the release:

"[Name], your oath is fulfilled. Your watch is ended. By Castor blood, be released."
  • Spoken by Akilles (or one he empowers), the sarcophagus goes silent, its cord withers.
  • Spoken by anyone without the blood, nothing happens.
  • Names come from Laeren or the effigies.

When all twelve are silent, the heart stutters, stops, and crumbles. The anchor is destroyed — and the urn can now be broken.

The hard way (with a cost): cut a cord (STR DC 14), but a king released without his name screams — necrotic pulse (DC 15 CON, 4d8, no healing down here). Twice wakes the Sentinel.

⛓️ The Silence of Kings (the core challenge)

The vault hoards power the way Vecna hoards secrets — the party's magic is choked, pulling an overpowered party down to solving things as people.

  • Throughout the vault: any spell of 4th level or higher requires a DC 18 spellcasting-ability check or it fizzles (slot wasted). Concentration saves at disadvantage.
  • In the Choir chamber: a full Antimagic Field. No spells, no magic items, no divine intervention — silence the Choir by voice and authority.
  • Describe spells going grey and sputtering out, not exploding.

💥 If They Try to Smash the Urn Early (plain rules)

  • While the soul-anchor is intact, the urn takes no damage — attacks and spells land like striking bedrock. Any fragment chipped off reattaches in seconds.
  • If they keep forcing it, the urn drinks the assault: each round they attack it, the reform clock drops by 1 day, and the cold wakes the Sentinel.
  • Bottom line: there's no brute-forcing it. Silence the Choir first.

🩸 The Pursuer Returns — Here, and Only Here

  • Context: Jimothy already lifted Akilles's Unseen Pursuer bane — he's been free of it. But the phylactery is the doppelganger's soul, so in this vault, as he bleeds, the thing he thought was gone reaches for him one last time.
  • The figure stands in his peripheral vision — his own face, crowned, cold — always one tier behind.
  • Vault-only effect (not the old bane): each time he sheds blood, DC 16 WIS save or shaken (−1d6) for 1 min, hearing: "You can't kill me. I'm the part of you that wanted the throne."
  • The reveal: that pursuer was always the doppelganger's soul reaching for him. Jimothy treated the symptom; the source is here. Breaking the urn ends it forever.

🏃 The Unmaking & The Collapse

Let Akilles break the urn.

You set your hand to the urn and it's only cold clay now. From inside, the crying stops. There's a single, soft sound — like a breath finally let go — and then nothing. It's over.

Then the vault dies. Run the escape as a group skill challenge — 4 successes before 3 failures:

  • Athletics/Acrobatics DC 14 — collapsing plinth-bridges, buckling stair.
  • Survival/Investigation DC 13 — the way up no longer matches the way down.
  • Strength DC 15 — hauling a companion (or Laeren) clear.
  • Magic works again once they clear the Choir's dead field — let them feel it return.

On 3 failures: no deaths — a character is briefly buried and hauled out battered. Breathlessness, not a TPK.

Feywild — Palace of Heart's Desire

Fifth Rod piece (Find the Path). Replaces "Night of Blue Fire." The whole chapter is the time-frozen palace of the archfey Zybilna — a bargain- and puzzle-heavy desire-maze where the party gets lost, with two sparse fights. LEVEL UP at end

Running This Chapter

The party reaches for the fifth piece, but Find the Path won't resolve — it points everywhere at once. The piece sits in Prismeer, a Feywild domain the Hourglass Coven shattered into three splinter-realms (Hither / Thither / Yon) and which, in the DFR, was never repaired. Its archfey, Zybilna, is frozen in time in her palace at the center. While she's frozen, the path stays scrambled. The rod drags the party to the one point where the three shards still touch — the palace gates. From there, the chapter is the palace.

RAW aside: Find the Path routes to fixed locations, not objects — so this is the Rod's pull, not the spell finding the piece. The spell itself loops because the palace folds space (the fixed-location requirement fails). This is intentional, not a fudge — say so if asked.

🩶 Olaf's Flashbacks (deliver privately to Wren)

Since the party freed the Lady of Pain, reality is "continually breaking," and Olaf gets flashes of the ORIGINAL world — the world before Vecna's Remaking, which he (uniquely) has no memory of. The palace's memory-magic makes it leak through. Keep these short, disorienting, and private (pass Wren a note).

  • H1 (Gates): the familiar voice greets everyone by name — and for Olaf it's a voice he almost knows, from a life he never lived. A street. A window. Gone.
  • H4 (Hall of Wants): his vision ("true self + a family of his own") flickers doubled — the wanted future, and underneath it, a real past that isn't the amnesiac cover story. He can't tell which is the lie.
  • H6 (Larder): a stolen thing on the shelf is his — an object from the original world he shouldn't recognize but does.
  • H8/H11 (memory-magic peaks): strongest here. As Zybilna's memories are restored, Olaf's break in the other direction — he sees the world that was, whole, for a heartbeat, and it does not include Vecna. Deeply destabilizing for a Vecna agent.

Mechanics option (if you want teeth): each flashback, DC 13 WIS save; on a fail Olaf is stunned/shaken for a beat and Wren must roleplay the intrusion. On a success he holds it together but keeps the image. Purely narrative also works.

🏛️ Palace Features (apply throughout)

  • It folds space. Scrying, teleport, and Find the Path all loop back — the party can't skip to Zybilna; they must walk the maze.
  • Getting lost. Don't map it; the halls rearrange to match emotion. The party advances through areas H1→H11 in order no matter which way they go.
  • The Bliss-Trap (failure state). A character who fully accepts what the palace offers (H4) stops trying to leave. Allies can pull them out. If the whole party accepts, the palace keeps them — a soft TPK by contentment. This, not monsters, is the real danger.

Feywild-meets-Vecna (the rot underneath). The palace is dying, and Vecna's Remaking has infected the dream: fey beauty keeps curdling. A feast of moths under glass; a fountain running with silver that whispers names of the dead; laughter that arrives a half-second before the joke. Roll or pick one curdle per area for texture.

The palace remembers. Cruelty here (smashing the songbird H7, forcing a seal H11, robbing the Larder H6) is noticed — each such act imposes a cumulative −1 (max −3) on the final H11 seal checks, and Zybilna's first words on waking reflect how they treated her home.

Keyed Areas

H1 · The Gates — Threshold of Wants
Three torn skies — a green swamp-dusk, a deep forest-gloom, a violet storm — all lean toward one pale palace where they meet. Its doors are open. A warm, familiar voice greets each of you by name, and behind the doors you each glimpse, for a moment, something you have wanted for a long time.

No combat. The palace welcomes each character by name and flickers their deepest want (foreshadows H4). It is kind — that's what makes it dangerous.

Feeling the pull (roll): the welcome tugs at what each character wants most. A character who succeeds on a DC 20 Wisdom saving throw feels that pull consciously — they sense the trap inside the kindness and instinctively fix on one thing they will not trade away, gaining advantage on the H4 temptation saves. A character who states aloud what they won't give up (roleplay, no roll) gets that advantage automatically. Everyone else walks in unguarded.

H2 · The Shifting Halls
The doors close without a sound. A corridor you just walked is a ballroom when you turn back; a staircase climbs to the room you left. The palace is rearranging itself around you, gently, as if trying to make you comfortable.

Running the halls (how to describe it)

Don't map this — narrate it as the palace reading the party's mood and reshaping to match. No matter which way they go, they reach H3. Beats to reach for:

  • They try to backtrack → the way they came is now a different room entirely (a nursery, a throne room, a garden). Doors they opened are walls; walls are doors.
  • Someone feels grief or longing → the walls warm into a half-remembered home; a smell they know; a voice down a hall that stops when they listen.
  • They argue or push → the hall narrows, doors multiply, and no exit looks the same twice — the palace gets anxious and cloying, trying harder to please them.
  • The windows show, in turn, three torn skies — a green swamp-dusk, a deep forest-gloom, a violet storm. That's Hither / Thither / Yon, the three shattered realms (seeds H6/H7/H8 and the seals).

🕯️ NPC — Pemberly Ashgrove, the Frozen Guest

Who she is: a minor courtier of Zybilna's court — an autumn eladrin noblewoman, caught by the freeze mid-curtsy, but a sliver of her is awake and her eyes track you. She doesn't know she's frozen; she thinks you've just arrived at the party.

Looks: tall and slender, faintly luminous skin gone grey at the edges; an amber-and-grey gown decayed to cobweb and frost; a small antlered circlet; one gloved hand still lifted in welcome. When she smiles a beat too long, the skull shows through — and she doesn't notice.

How to play her: gracious, warm, a little frightened underneath, and looping — she repeats her greeting if the conversation stalls ("You've come such a long way — let me take your… oh, you've come such a long way"). Melancholy autumn-court gentility. No stat block needed (treat as a Noble/commoner if it ever matters); she won't fight.

What she gives (soft hints, freely):

  • "The little bird upstairs — she used to sing when someone was kind." → nursery / brown seal (H7/H11).
  • "Don't linger in the rooms that give you what you want. The ones who stay… stay." → the H4 bliss-trap warning.
  • "The lady of the house is only stuck because she wouldn't take a bad bargain. Stubborn thing." → Zybilna / violet seal (H8/H11).

The ache: she can't be freed until Zybilna wakes (H11). The party can't save her yet — a small, deliberate note of helplessness.

H3 · The Gallery of Small Cruelties
A long gallery of portraits in tarnished frames. The painted people are all caught in a single unkind instant, and as you pass, their eyes slide to follow you — and you feel them weighing what you've done.

The portraits (describe these — pick as you pass)

  • A mother's open hand frozen an inch from a small child's cheek; both faces turned away from each other, forever.
  • A man in a doorway, back turned and leaving; behind him a woman's hand is half-raised to stop him. The door will never finish closing.
  • A long feast table where every mouth is open in laughter — all of it aimed at one guest whose face is just beginning to crumple.
  • A grave being filled in, and a small hooded figure kept too far back to reach it, one hand out.
  • Two children: one holding a toy just out of the other's reach; the smaller one's face is pure, complete betrayal.
  • The only portrait that moves: a hooded, three-faced painter (grandmother, mother, maiden) working at an easel, brush frozen mid-stroke. This is the Hourglass Coven — they paint the court's worst moments to keep Prismeer ashamed and pliant (foreshadows Secret #1, H8).

The memory-toll (trap)

Meeting a portrait's eyes too long, or looting a frame → DC 18 Wisdom save. On a fail, the palace lifts a small kind memory (cosmetic/roleplay — they forget a friend's face for the scene) and the character has disadvantage on their first H4 temptation save (the palace now knows a soft spot). Passing without engaging: no toll.

H4 · The Hall of Wants (the temptations)
A series of small, perfect rooms — one for each of you. In each, the thing you most want is waiting, and it is real, and it is yours, if you will only stay.

The rooms (describe the WHERE — let players say the WHAT)

Each character steps alone into a small, perfect room. Don't tell them what they want — describe the setting and ask them to fill it in. Prompts:

  • Akilles — a throne room at dawn, warm light, every seat filled and every face turned to him, someone's hand on his shoulder. "Who's in the seats? What's been undone?"
  • Marianne — a small, warm home, a table set for the family, a familiar voice humming in the next room. "Who do you hear? Who sets the third place?" (Brutal, post-Silverymoon.)
  • Jimothy — a quiet shore at low tide, someone he couldn't save sitting whole and unbothered, patting the sand beside them. "Who's waiting for you? What do they say?"
  • Zephyr — Letherna with no leash: the pronged belt lying open and cold on a table, a door standing unlocked. "What does freedom look like — the belt gone, or the years given back?"
  • Olaf — a life that is simply, truly his, a threshold with a name over the door that he almost recognizes. "Whose life is it? What's the name?" (Run the doubled-vision flashback here — see the Olaf lock above.)

Refusing — and what happens if they can't

To leave: name the vision false and let it go (roleplay), or make a WIS or CHA save, DC 18 (advantage if they guarded something at H1).

Success: they let it go and move on — no penalty.

Failure = the vision takes hold: the character is Charmed by the room — they won't voluntarily leave and take no action against it. They linger. At the start of their next turn still lingering, the desire hardens into a Heart's Shadow (⚔️ Combat #1): a hostile mirror of the PC at half their HP, using their own stats. While the Shadow lives, the charmed PC is incapacitated (lost in the dream).

Breaking free: defeat the Shadow, or an ally reaches them — an action plus a DC 15 Charisma (Persuasion) to call them back, or bodily dragging them out (contested Strength; the charmed PC resists the first time). Either ends the charm and the Shadow collapses.

The real danger: if the whole party fails and no one breaks free, the palace simply keeps them — a soft TPK by contentment. This, not monsters, is the chapter's actual threat. Make sure at least one save or ally-pull lands early so they feel how it works.

Mood: Cold, stark, oppressive order. Think totalitarian regime — East Berlin, not Mordor. Everything is clean and controlled but devoid of joy.

Music suggestions: Minimalist tension — Chernobyl OST, Blade Runner 2049 ambient, sparse piano. No drones or horror cues.

🌑 Read Aloud — Emerging from the Alley

You step out of the alley and into cold, stark daylight. Waterdeep stretches before you under a pale, washed-out sky — not the perpetual twilight you might have expected, but something worse. The city looks almost normal. Almost clean. The cobblestones are scrubbed. The buildings stand straight and well-maintained. Banners bearing Vecna's eye hang at precise intervals, crisp and unwrinkled.

But no one laughs. A woman hurries past clutching a basket, eyes fixed on the ground. Two soldiers in polished armor patrol the far end of the street — they don't shamble or moan. They walk with discipline, scanning faces. A shopkeeper sweeps his stoop in silence, glancing up at you for one instant too long before looking away. The air is bitterly cold. This is a city that has learned to be afraid.

Here and there, you notice something strange — cracks in the air itself, shimmering with warm golden light. Through one, you catch a fleeting glimpse of a crowded market stall, a merchant laughing with a customer. Then it's gone, and the silence settles back in.
