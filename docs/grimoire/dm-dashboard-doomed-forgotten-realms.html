⚰️ The Vault of Ended Kings

Destroy Algar's phylactery so he can't reform. A quiet, dread-soaked delve — mostly RP and puzzle, very little combat, and a hard scramble out as the vault collapses. The catch: the vault smothers magic, so the party's overwhelming power is, for once, off the table.

⏳ The Clock

🗺️ The Plan (the simple version)

When the party asks "okay, how do we actually do this?", it's three steps:

  1. Find it. The phylactery hides among hundreds of identical urns. Akilles's blood makes the real one cry out — they find it by the sound.
  2. It won't break yet. The true urn is protected by a soul-anchor (the Choir) below. While the anchor is intact, the urn can't be damaged at all.
  3. Break the anchor, then the urn. Silence the twelve dead kings of the Choir (each king's name + Akilles's word of release) to destroy the anchor. Then smash the urn — Algar is gone for good.

The Layout (a simple vertical stack)

  1. The throne room (where they are) → hidden stair behind the dais.
  2. The Hall of the Ended Kings — entry hall of dead-king statues.
  3. The Gallery of Urns — the big tiered chamber where the phylactery hides.
  4. The Choir of Ended Kings — the soul-anchor chamber, directly below the Gallery.

Mood: Held-breath cold. A place that eats sound. This is where the party's power matters least and their choices matter most.

Walkthrough

1 · The Hidden Descent
The throne room is silent now. Algar is gone — when he fell, his whole body collapsed into a drift of grey dust that still hangs in the cracked, settling air. Where the throne stood, a seam of cold blue light has traced itself across the floor in the shape of a crown.
  • The walls are cracked from the fight; fine grey dust (all that's left of Algar) drifts in the light.
  • A deep cold rises from the seam.
  • Shove the throne aside, or Investigation DC 13, to open the stair.
2 · Laeren, Keeper of the Vault (RP heart)

Found in the Undercroft beneath the Dread Market — the last hereditary Keeper of the Vault, who has served the royal house his whole life.

Laeren only knows the Doomed Forgotten Realms. To him there has only ever been one Algar: the cold, lichdom-seeking King Algar he's served and feared for years. He has no concept of other universes or of a good Algar. Akilles wears the king's exact face, and Laeren can't tell them apart.

Lore reminder: Akilles is Algar — the version from the regular Realms who killed their father Aldar to stop his lichdom. This world's Algar followed Aldar into it. Same man, two universes.

Showing Akilles's face is dangerous — Laeren sees his king and reacts with terror. Two ways to run it:

  • Path A — Hide the face (recommended). Disguise Akilles (Crown of Lies / hood / keep him back) and approach as outsiders who mean to end King Algar. Worn down by years under a monster, Laeren can be persuaded to help.
  • Path B — Wear the fear. Let Laeren believe the king stands before him and lean on the terror. Darkly ironic — the Keeper helps "the king" reach the jar that ends him. Manage the questions carefully.
An old man sits with his back to a dead shrine, sharpening a knife he is far too frail to use. When strangers step in, he tenses. "...The vault's sealed. There's nothing for you. Please. I'm only the Keeper." (If Akilles's face is seen:) The knife clatters to the floor. "M-my king— I've kept it, I've kept everything, just as you— please don't—" He cannot conceive you are anyone but the man whose vault this is.

What he gives (if earned):

  • The phylactery hides among the urns; only the royal blood (Algar's own — which Akilles carries) finds it, by making the true urn cry out.
  • The urn can't break until the soul-anchor (the Choir) below is destroyed.
  • Destroy the anchor by naming and releasing the twelve dead kings — only the royal Castor blood can speak it. Laeren knows all twelve names and the words.
  • The vault smothers magic. "Go down as men, not as wizards."

Getting it out of him: Path A — roleplay (promise to end the king, mercy), spend a secret, or Persuasion DC 16. Path B — careful kingly demands. Cruelty makes him clam up.

Akilles's blood works disguised or not (the Crown changes looks, not blood). His fate is the party's: left in the Undercroft, brought to Sigil (recurring ally), or found dead later. He does not enter the vault.

3 · The Hall of the Ended Kings

Below the stair: dozens of stone effigies of kneeling kings, each holding out its own crown, every face chiseled blank.

Who are these kings? For as long as this doomed Sword Coast has existed, it's been ruled by a line of kings who each swore themselves to Vecna for power and a promise of immortality. When each died, the cult stored his death here — and those hoarded deaths let the current king cheat death and reform. The urns ahead are that long line of spent kings. Algar is just the latest.

  • The blanked faces still carry regnal marks and a carved name at each base (the names needed below — History/Investigation DC 15, or get them from Laeren).
  • An inscription repeats above them: ENDED, BUT NOT RELEASED.
4 · The Gallery of Urns — find it by sound
The hall opens into a vast round chamber, tiered like an amphitheater turned inward. On every plinth stands an urn: bonewhite, lidless, identical. Hundreds of them, climbing into dark you can't see the top of.

The true phylactery is one urn among hundreds, with no way to tell them apart by sight (even Detect Magic shows the whole room the same uniform necromancy).

  • When Akilles sheds a drop of blood, one urn begins to cry — a faint, continuous weeping sound (his own voice, if you want to twist the knife). Nothing changes visibly. They locate it by ear.
  • The crying fades if he stops bleeding — he must keep a fresh drop flowing while they cross to it (Perception to pinpoint in the echo).

For Akilles's personal haunting as he bleeds, see "The Pursuer" below.

5 · The Choir of Ended Kings (the soul-anchor)
A round vault, domed in black stone. Twelve stone sarcophagi ring the edge — the twelve greatest of the dead kings. From each, a cord of braided bone and grey sinew runs inward to a heart of black obsidian, fist-sized, floating, beating once every few seconds. With every beat the twelve sarcophagi hum a single low note together — a choir with no breath.

Plainly: the obsidian heart is the soul-anchor. While it beats, the phylactery upstairs cannot be damaged. To destroy it, release all twelve kings, one at a time.

The word of release. The vault answers to the royal Castor blood, which Akilles carries (he is Algar, same blood). Speak the king's name + the release:

"[Name], your oath is fulfilled. Your watch is ended. By Castor blood, be released."
  • Spoken by Akilles (or one he empowers), the sarcophagus goes silent, its cord withers.
  • Spoken by anyone without the blood, nothing happens.
  • Names come from Laeren or the effigies.

When all twelve are silent, the heart stutters, stops, and crumbles. The anchor is destroyed — and the urn can now be broken.

The hard way (with a cost): cut a cord (STR DC 14), but a king released without his name screams — necrotic pulse (DC 15 CON, 4d8, no healing down here). Twice wakes the Sentinel.

⛓️ The Silence of Kings (the core challenge)

The vault hoards power the way Vecna hoards secrets — the party's magic is choked, pulling an overpowered party down to solving things as people.

  • Throughout the vault: any spell of 4th level or higher requires a DC 18 spellcasting-ability check or it fizzles (slot wasted). Concentration saves at disadvantage.
  • In the Choir chamber: a full Antimagic Field. No spells, no magic items, no divine intervention — silence the Choir by voice and authority.
  • Describe spells going grey and sputtering out, not exploding.

💥 If They Try to Smash the Urn Early (plain rules)

  • While the soul-anchor is intact, the urn takes no damage — attacks and spells land like striking bedrock. Any fragment chipped off reattaches in seconds.
  • If they keep forcing it, the urn drinks the assault: each round they attack it, the reform clock drops by 1 day, and the cold wakes the Sentinel.
  • Bottom line: there's no brute-forcing it. Silence the Choir first.

🩸 The Pursuer Returns — Here, and Only Here

  • Context: Jimothy already lifted Akilles's Unseen Pursuer bane — he's been free of it. But the phylactery is the doppelganger's soul, so in this vault, as he bleeds, the thing he thought was gone reaches for him one last time.
  • The figure stands in his peripheral vision — his own face, crowned, cold — always one tier behind.
  • Vault-only effect (not the old bane): each time he sheds blood, DC 16 WIS save or shaken (−1d6) for 1 min, hearing: "You can't kill me. I'm the part of you that wanted the throne."
  • The reveal: that pursuer was always the doppelganger's soul reaching for him. Jimothy treated the symptom; the source is here. Breaking the urn ends it forever.

🏃 The Unmaking & The Collapse

Let Akilles break the urn.

You set your hand to the urn and it's only cold clay now. From inside, the crying stops. There's a single, soft sound — like a breath finally let go — and then nothing. It's over.

Then the vault dies. Run the escape as a group skill challenge — 4 successes before 3 failures:

  • Athletics/Acrobatics DC 14 — collapsing plinth-bridges, buckling stair.
  • Survival/Investigation DC 13 — the way up no longer matches the way down.
  • Strength DC 15 — hauling a companion (or Laeren) clear.
  • Magic works again once they clear the Choir's dead field — let them feel it return.

On 3 failures: no deaths — a character is briefly buried and hauled out battered. Breathlessness, not a TPK.

🌸 Feywild — Palace of Heart's Desire

Rod Piece #5 (Find the Path). A villain-free arc: the threat is temptation, entropy, and a memory puzzle — not a big bad. Replaces "Night of Blue Fire on Krynn."

Mood: Aching, beautiful, dangerous. Shifting halls that respond to emotion. Every room offers you the thing you most want — and means it.

Premise

Rod piece 4's attunement points the portal to the Feywild. Zybilna's (Iggwilv / Tasha's) palace has fallen into chaos as her fractured memories spiral it out of control, and the fifth Rod fragment is trapped in the heart of the volatile maze. To reach it, the party helps Zybilna regain control by reassembling her lost memories — including the truth that her son Iuz unwittingly enabled Vecna's conquest.

💔 The Desire-Maze — Per-PC Temptations

The palace reshapes to each PC's deepest desire — a vision offering it, "real" if only they stay:

  • Akilles — the doomed timeline undone: crowned, brother safe, father redeemed and alive.
  • Olaf — his true self / Vecna's favor. Dangerous for his secret — the palace surfaces "before" flashes he's begun getting since the Lady of Pain's freeing. A live wire for Wren.
  • Jimothy — his murdered uncle returned, or Ehud back, the betrayal-guilt erased.
  • Marianne — Bronte & Marion whole and happy. Devastating if run after Silverymoon.
  • Zephyr — freedom from the pronged belt and the Raven Queen's service, or his stolen years and his ancestors' redemption.

🧩 Memory Restoration

Stabilizing the palace (and reaching the piece) requires gathering Zybilna's shattered memory-shards, scattered through the desire-halls — each usually claimed only by rejecting the temptation it's hidden inside. Assembling them reveals the Iuz secret (his failed strike with a forged Sword of Kas became Vecna's path toward Sigil). Huge leverage: Alustriel does not know Iuz's role.

⚔️ Difficulty (no villain, still deadly)

  • The maze as hazard: shifting geometry, localized reverse-gravity, emotion-fueled wild-magic surges, illusions that deal real damage if believed.
  • Heart's Shadows: a PC who lingers in a temptation spawns a mirror-duplicate of themselves — fighting versions of their own over-tuned builds is one of the few fair challenges for this party.
  • Emotion-locked doors: passage requires genuinely feeling/expressing, not rolling.
  • Bliss-trap (failure state): fail the tests and the palace simply keeps you, content — a soft TPK by contentment.

Climax & Rewards

The heart of the maze yields the piece only once the party helps Zybilna accept the painful Iuz memory instead of fleeing into desire.

Reward: Rod piece 5 (Find the Path); Zybilna restored as an ally (a Wizard of the Three); the Iuz secret; possibly Tasha recovering some lost magic. LEVEL UP

💀 Waterdeep (Doomed Sword Coast)

Mood: Cold, stark, oppressive order. Think totalitarian regime — East Berlin, not Mordor. Everything is clean and controlled but devoid of joy.

Music suggestions: Minimalist tension — Chernobyl OST, Blade Runner 2049 ambient, sparse piano. No drones or horror cues.

Sensory: Bitter cold midday air, pale washed-out sunlight, scrubbed cobblestones, silence broken only by boot-steps. No laughter, no street musicians, no children playing. The smell of lye and stone.

🌑 Read Aloud — Emerging from the Alley

You step out of the alley and into cold, stark daylight. Waterdeep stretches before you under a pale, washed-out sky — not the perpetual twilight you might have expected, but something worse. The city looks almost normal. Almost clean. The cobblestones are scrubbed. The buildings stand straight and well-maintained. Banners bearing Vecna's eye hang at precise intervals, crisp and unwrinkled.

But no one laughs. A woman hurries past clutching a basket, eyes fixed on the ground. Two soldiers in polished armor patrol the far end of the street — they don't shamble or moan. They walk with discipline, scanning faces. A shopkeeper sweeps his stoop in silence, glancing up at you for one instant too long before looking away. The air is bitterly cold. This is a city that has learned to be afraid.

Here and there, you notice something strange — cracks in the air itself, shimmering with warm golden light. Through one, you catch a fleeting glimpse of a crowded market stall, a merchant laughing with a customer. Then it's gone, and the silence settles back in.

🆕 Crevices of Dusk

  • Planar rifts from the Lady of Pain's return — show glimpses of "old" Waterdeep (warmth, laughter, color)
  • The regime treats these as "reality malfunctions" — official posters warn citizens to report them
  • Soldiers cordon off Crevice areas; anyone caught staring or talking about them is taken for "re-education"
  • Some merchants near the Dread Market are starting to remember — dangerous knowledge in a surveillance state

Key Locations

🏰 Castle Waterdeep

King Algar's seat of power. Imposing and immaculate — polished stone, disciplined guards in formation, banners hung with geometric precision. It feels less like a haunted castle and more like a dictator's palace.

  • Throne room converted into a phylactery chamber (but the phylactery itself is NOT here)
  • Heavy guard: disciplined undead soldiers, wraith sentinels, cult advisors in formal robes
  • Lair actions active in throne room (see Boss Fight page)
  • Captain Voss patrols the approach — professional, not monstrous
🏪 The Dread Market

An orderly open-air market — stalls arranged in rigid rows, prices state-controlled, inspectors walking the aisles. The name is ironic; there's nothing chaotic about it. Best place for info gathering if you're careful.

  • Plainclothes informants blend with shoppers — DC 16 Perception to spot one watching you
  • Some merchants near Crevices are starting to remember "before" — whispered conversations, quickly hushed
  • Mireva can be found here — she keeps a curio stall near a recurring Crevice
⚰️ The Boneyard (formerly the City of the Dead)

Waterdeep's ancient cemetery district, repurposed under Algar's regime. The tombs have been catalogued and sealed; the grounds are maintained with unsettling precision. Undead laborers tend the paths in silence.

  • Functions as a recruitment center — the regime raises loyal undead from noble crypts here
  • Warded perimeter; entering without authorization draws immediate patrol response
  • Rumored to house a secondary ritual site for Vecna's priests deep beneath the old mausoleums
  • Note: The ritual the party disrupted in Session 1 was in Neverwinter (now Evernight), far north on the Sword Coast — not here.
⚓ The Docks

Functioning and efficient — every vessel logged, every cargo manifest filed. Departure requires stamped papers bearing Vecna's seal. Could be an escape route, but smugglers here are extremely cautious; they operate on a trust-or-die basis.

⛪ The Undercroft (Temple of Tyr ruins)

Resistance hideout beneath the old Temple of Tyr.

  • Brother Solace operates from here
  • Hidden entrance through collapsed crypt
  • Small cell: 8-12 members
  • DC 15 Persuasion or proof of opposing Vecna to gain trust

🔮 Mireva — Dread Market Merchant

What she knows:
