# Chapter: The Palace of Heart's Desire (Feywild)

*Replaces "Night of Blue Fire." To retrieve the fifth piece of the Rod of Seven Parts, the characters must enter the time-frozen palace of the archfey Zybilna, get lost in its desire-maze, and free her. The whole chapter is the palace — bargain- and puzzle-heavy, with two sparse fights.*

## Running This Chapter

This chapter begins after the characters retrieve the fourth Rod piece and reach for the fifth — but **Find the Path won't resolve.** It points everywhere at once. The piece sits in **Prismeer**, a Feywild domain the **Hourglass Coven** long ago shattered into three splinter-realms (Hither, Thither, Yon) and which, in the Doomed Forgotten Realms, was **never repaired.** Its archfey, **Zybilna**, is **frozen in time** in her palace at the dead center where the three realms used to join. While she is frozen, the path stays scrambled.

The characters don't tour the three realms — the rod drags them to **the one point where the three shards still touch: the palace gates.** From there, the chapter is the palace.

## Character Advancement

The characters should be 15th level. They **gain a level** after retrieving the fifth Rod piece in **area H8**.

## Power of Secrets

The characters can learn **two secrets** here (one each from two NPCs):

- **How Prismeer Broke** — from **Tib Tatterglass** (area **H6**).
- **Iuz's Role** — from **Zybilna** (area **H8**).

## Fifth Rod Piece

The fifth piece (Find the Path) is in **area H8**, given by Zybilna when she is freed.

> ### DM — Who Zybilna Is (and Isn't)
> **Zybilna is NOT the Tasha the party knows.** Tasha of the Wizards Three is a *past version of herself* that Zybilna sent to Alustriel — a powerful wizard, not yet a witch-queen or archfey. Zybilna is the full archfey: once Natasha (raised by Baba Yaga), then **Iggwilv the Witch Queen**, now an archfey turning Fey. She is the **mother of Iuz.** Mordenkainen and Alustriel have no doubles in the DFR.
>
> **DM seed — Tasha is pregnant** (by Olaf, a few days; only you know). **Optional, time-based hint:** the longer the party takes in the palace, the more real time passes outside — so if they dawdle or get badly lost, Zybilna can sense the new thread when freed (see H8). If they're quick, leave it offscreen.

## Palace Features

Unless a room says otherwise, these apply throughout:

- **It folds space.** Scrying, teleportation, and Find the Path all **loop back** to where they started. The party cannot skip to Zybilna — they must walk the maze.
- **Getting lost.** You don't map the palace; its halls rearrange to match the emotions of whoever walks them. The party advances through the **keyed areas in order (H1→H8)** no matter which direction they choose — describe the palace endlessly rebuilding around them.
- **The Bliss-Trap (failure state).** Any character who fully accepts what the palace offers (area H3) stops trying to leave — they are *content*. Allies can pull them out (an emotional appeal, or breaking the illusion). If the **whole party** accepts, the palace simply keeps them: a soft TPK by contentment. This, not monsters, is the real danger.
- **Light & sound.** Warm gold light from nowhere; sound is soft and close, like a held breath.

# H1. The Gates — Threshold of Wants

> *Three torn skies — a green swamp-dusk, a deep forest-gloom, a violet storm — all lean toward one place: a pale palace standing alone where they meet. Its doors are already open. A warm voice, kind and familiar, greets each of you by name, and behind the doors you each glimpse, just for a moment, something you have wanted for a long time.*

**Contents.** No combat. The palace welcomes each character personally, showing each a flicker of their deepest want (foreshadowing H3). Set the tone: this place is *kind*, and that is exactly what makes it dangerous.

**Steeling yourself.** A character who states aloud something they refuse to trade away here has advantage on the tests in **H3**.

---

# H2. The Shifting Halls

> *The doors close without a sound. The halls beyond do not stay still — a corridor you just walked is a ballroom when you turn back; a staircase climbs to the room you left. The palace is rearranging itself around you, gently, as if trying to make you comfortable.*

**Contents.** This is the connective tissue and where you teach the **getting-lost** rule (above). No matter which way they go, they arrive at **H3.** Let them feel the wrongness of a place that won't hold still — and the pull to simply stop and rest (foreshadow the bliss-trap).

---

# H3. The Hall of Wants

> *The hall opens into a series of small, perfect rooms — one for each of you. In each, the thing you most want is waiting, and it is real, and it is yours, if you will only stay.*

**Contents.** The palace reshapes to each PC's deepest desire and offers it. Run these as short personal scenes — bargain and roleplay, not dice:

- **Akilles** — the doomed timeline undone: crowned, his brother safe, his father redeemed and alive.
- **Marianne** — **Bronte and Marion, whole and happy.** *(If Silverymoon already happened, the palace offers back exactly what he just lost.)*
- **Jimothy** — his murdered uncle returned, or Ehud back, his guilt erased.
- **Zephyr** — freedom from the Raven Queen's service and the pronged belt, or his stolen years restored.
- **Olaf** — his true self and a family of his own. *(Optional pregnancy seed: the vision includes a child he doesn't yet know exists.)*

**Passing the gate.** A character moves on by **refusing** the offer — naming it as false and letting it go. Roleplay it, or call for a **Wisdom or Charisma save, DC 18.**

> **Sparse Combat #1 — Heart's Shadow.** A character who *lingers* (fails / won't let go) hardens their desire into a **Heart's Shadow**: a hostile mirror of that PC. Use the PC's own statistics at **half hit points.** Defeating the Shadow — or being pulled free by an ally — frees the character. Only triggers for characters who linger; a party that refuses cleanly skips this fight.

# H4. The Larder of Lost Things

> *A long pantry of shelves climbing out of sight, crammed with everything the palace has ever taken in: single shoes, wedding rings, baby teeth, a stoppered jar of someone's laughter. Everything here belonged to someone, once.*

**Contents.** A quiet bargain/puzzle room (echoes **Hither**, the hoarder's realm). The shelves hold harmless oddments and a few real treasures. Nothing here is guarded, but the palace "notices" what is taken.

**The key item.** Among the hoard is the **Magpie's Coin** — a bright stolen coin that hums faintly. It is the kind of *taken, hoarded thing* the **green seal** in H8 will demand be **given away freely.** Clever players grab it; if they don't, they can give up something of their own at H8 instead.

**Treasure.** A *Potion of Greater Healing* ×2; a small bag of cut gems (250 gp); a single earring that whispers the wearer's name when danger is near (minor trinket, advantage on initiative once per day — adjust to taste).

---

# H5. The Broken Nursery

> *A child's playroom gone to rot. Toys lie everywhere — dolls with the stuffing pulled out, a rocking horse with its legs snapped, a wind-up soldier ticking slower and slower. In the middle, a clockwork songbird lies on its back, one wing bent, trying and failing to sing.*

**Contents.** A poignant puzzle room (echoes **Thither**, the cruel nursery-hag's realm). The **clockwork songbird** is broken and suffering in its small way.

**The key act.** A character can **mend or free the songbird** — Tinker's Tools / Sleight of Hand **DC 15**, a *Mending* cantrip, or simply releasing it gently. A mended/freed songbird becomes the **act of care** the **brown seal** in H8 requires; it perches on a shoulder and leads the way (advantage to avoid getting lost from here on). Smashing or ignoring it is remembered by the palace (DM's discretion — a small penalty at H8).

# H6. The Faded Wing — Tib Tatterglass

> *This dead-end isn't one — the wall keeps almost-being a door. A satyr in a faded jester's motley sits against it, and he flickers: here, then gone, then here, like a candle in a draft. He has been fading for a very long time. When he sees you, he laughs, and it is the saddest sound you've heard.*

**Contents.** **Tib Tatterglass**, court-jester of Zybilna, caught at the edge of the time-freeze and slowly fading from existence. He is lonely, mischievous, terrified, and still loyal to his frozen mistress. He **witnessed the Coven break Prismeer and freeze her.** He knows the way onward and he holds **Secret #1.** Being Fey, everything he gives comes at a **bargain with a catch.**

**Tib's prices (locked).**
- **For directions / a shortcut past the worst of the maze:** he asks each character he helps for **one happy memory of home.** Whoever pays **permanently loses that specific memory** (pick a small backstory detail; roleplay the cost). He breathes it in and steadies, a little less faded.
- **For the full secret and the clue he holds:** he asks for **a future favor — an open-ended debt** he (or whatever he becomes) may call in later. *(A deliberate recurring hook. If you'd rather, the alternative price is a character's true name, freely given — dangerous in the Fey way.)*
- **The free path:** if the party **sincerely vows to free Zybilna** and means it, Tib helps for nothing, weeping, asking only one thing: *"Tell her Tib waited."*

> ### 🔒 Secret #1 — How Prismeer Broke
> Tib tells it plainly: the **Hourglass Coven — three hags — shattered Prismeer into Hither, Thither, and Yon and froze Zybilna in time** to seize her domain while she was vulnerable. Learning the true tale counts as a **secret** (Power of Secrets).

**The clue he gives (for H8).** Tib also knows **what Zybilna was doing when she was frozen:** she was **refusing the Coven's bargain** — choosing Prismeer and her people over surrendering her power. This is the **"true ending"** the **violet seal** in H8 will demand be spoken aloud.

# H7. Antechamber of the Stopped Clock

> *A round antechamber of pale marble. At its center stands a knight in beautiful, archaic armor, utterly still — frosted with a fine grey dust, frozen like everything else here. Beyond it, a single door, edged in cold light. As you step toward the door, the knight's head turns with a sound like a second hand ticking.*

**Contents.** The palace's lone guardian. As the party approaches the inner door, the **Hour-Frozen Knight** wakes to bar the way. **This is sparse combat #2** — one set-piece fight, then done.

> ### Hour-Frozen Knight
> *Medium undead (time-touched), lawful neutral — a knight the Coven froze and bound to guard the inner door.*
> **AC** 18 (plate + shield) · **HP** 207 (18d8+126) · **Speed** 30 ft.
> **STR** 20 (+5) · **DEX** 11 (+0) · **CON** 24 (+7) · **INT** 11 (+0) · **WIS** 16 (+3) · **CHA** 15 (+2)
> **Saving Throws** Con +13, Wis +9 · **Damage Resistances** necrotic; nonmagical B/P/S · **Condition Immunities** charmed, exhaustion, frightened, paralyzed · **Senses** darkvision 60 ft. · **CR** 14
> **Out of Time.** The knight doesn't roll initiative — it always acts on **count 20 and count 10** of every round (two turns per round). It can't be surprised.
> **Rewind (1/short rest, reaction).** When reduced to 0 HP, the knight instead drops to **half its maximum HP** as the last few seconds rewind — once.
> **Actions.** *Multiattack.* Two **Frostbound Greatsword** attacks: +11 to hit, reach 5 ft., 14 (2d10+5) slashing plus 9 (2d8) cold, and the target's speed is halved until the end of its next turn.
> *Stilling Gaze (recharge 5–6).* One creature the knight can see makes a **DC 17 Wisdom save** or is **paralyzed** until the end of its next turn, frozen for a heartbeat out of time.
>
> *(Want more than one fight's worth? Add 2–3 "clockwork wisps" — will-o'-wisp stats — but it's built to stand alone.)*

**Treasure.** The knight's armor and **Frostbound Greatsword** (a *+1 greatsword* that deals an extra 1d8 cold) can be claimed after the fight.

# H8. The Stopped Clock — Zybilna's Chamber

> *A still, white chamber under a frozen sky. In its center stands a woman mid-step — dark-haired, crowned in antlers and starlight, caught in the act of turning to face something behind her. She does not breathe. Beside her, taller than a person, stands a great hourglass full of glowing sand that does not fall. This is the heart of the palace, and the heart of the silence.*

**Contents.** **Zybilna**, frozen mid-defiance, and the **Coven's hourglass** holding her out of time. Freeing her is the chapter's climax — a puzzle, not a fight.

> ### The Hourglass — The Three Seals (locked solution)
> The hourglass is bound by three colored seals, one for each splinter-realm. **Smashing it just resets it.** To drain the stolen time and free Zybilna, the party must open all three:
>
> - **🟢 Green seal (Hither / greed).** Place a **taken, hoarded thing on the pan and give it away freely** — the **Magpie's Coin** from H4, or a real treasure of their own surrendered with no expectation. Greed-magic releases when met with genuine generosity.
> - **🟤 Brown seal (Thither / cruelty to small things).** Present the **mended/freed clockwork songbird** from H5 (or perform an act of care here — mend something broken, free something trapped). Releases on kindness.
> - **🟣 Violet seal (Yon / the unfinished story).** **Speak Zybilna's true ending aloud** — that, when frozen, she was *refusing the Coven and choosing Prismeer over her own power* (the clue from Tib, H6). Releases on truth.
>
> **When all three open,** the sand runs out, time resumes, the palace stops shifting, and the three realms begin — slowly — to lean back toward each other.
>
> **Getting it wrong (time-lash, hazard not monster).** Forcing a seal, or speaking a false ending, triggers a burst of unmade time: each creature in the room makes a **DC 16 Constitution save** or loses its next turn as a few seconds rewind (and, cosmetically, ages a few years). No combat — just a cost for guessing.

**Zybilna freed.** She wakes mid-turn, finishes the step she began, and finds her enemies long gone and her domain in pieces. She is grateful — in the cool Fey way, with strings.

> ### 🔒 Secret #2 — Iuz's Role
> Zybilna reveals what she has carried alone: her son **Iuz** attacked Vecna with a **forged Sword of Kas**; the attack failed and became Vecna's **path to Sigil**, nearly unmaking the multiverse. **Alustriel never knew it was Iuz.** This counts as a **secret**, and it is major leverage.

**Rewards.**
- The **fifth Rod piece (Find the Path)**, which now resolves cleanly for the first time. **Rod 5/7.**
- A **Feywild boon** of your choosing (e.g., Zybilna grants each character one *Wish*-lite favor she'll honor later, or a charm of the archfey — adjust to taste).
- *(Optional, if the party was slow — the pregnancy hint:)* Zybilna studies them and murmurs that "a new thread has been spun among your wizards — small, and very new," and says no more.
- **LEVEL UP.**

# Appendix — Quick Map (logical, not spatial)

Because the palace folds space, run it as a **sequence of gates**, not a grid:

**H1 Gates** → **H2 Shifting Halls** → **H3 Hall of Wants** *(Heart's Shadow if they linger)* → **H4 Larder** *(Magpie's Coin)* → **H5 Broken Nursery** *(songbird)* → **H6 Tib's Wing** *(Secret #1 + the "true ending" clue)* → **H7 Antechamber** *(Hour-Frozen Knight)* → **H8 Stopped Clock** *(Three Seals → free Zybilna → Secret #2 → fifth Rod piece → level up)*.

The three seal-solutions are seeded earlier on purpose: **Coin (H4) → green**, **Songbird (H5) → brown**, **Tib's clue (H6) → violet.** A party that explores is rewarded with an easy finish; a party that rushes can still improvise at the cost of a time-lash or two.
