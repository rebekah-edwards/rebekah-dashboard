# The Vault of Ended Kings

*A short chapter. The party has just slain King Algar in Castle Waterdeep — but a lich destroyed without his phylactery only reforms. Beneath the throne, his soul-jar waits. This is a quiet, dread-soaked delve: very little fighting, a lot of RP and puzzle, and a hard scramble out as the vault collapses. The catch — the vault smothers magic, so the party's overwhelming power is, for once, off the table.*

**The clock.** Algar will reform in **7 days** (rolled) unless the phylactery is destroyed first.

---

## The Plan (the simple version)

> When the party asks "okay, how do we actually do this?", this is the whole quest in three steps. Give it to them through Laeren, or let them piece it together.

1. **Find it.** The phylactery is hidden among hundreds of identical urns. **Akilles's blood** makes the real one *cry out* — the party finds it by the sound.
2. **It won't break yet.** The true urn is protected by a **soul-anchor** (the Choir) in the chamber below. While the anchor is intact, the urn cannot be damaged at all.
3. **Break the anchor, then the urn.** Silence the twelve dead kings of the Choir (speak each one's name + Akilles's *word of release*) to destroy the anchor. Then the urn can be smashed — and Algar is gone for good.

## The Layout

A simple vertical stack, top to bottom — no battle map needed:

1. **The throne room** (where they are) → a hidden stair behind the dais.
2. **The Hall of the Ended Kings** — an entry hall of dead-king statues.
3. **The Gallery of Urns** — the big tiered chamber where the phylactery hides.
4. **The Choir of Ended Kings** — the soul-anchor chamber, directly below the Gallery.

---

# 1. The Hidden Descent

*The throne room is silent now. Algar is gone — when he fell, his whole body collapsed into a drift of grey dust that still hangs in the cracked, settling air. Where the throne stood, a seam of cold blue light has traced itself across the floor in the shape of a crown.*

The throne conceals a spiral stair down. Flavor to sprinkle as you like:

- The walls are **cracked** from the fight; fine grey dust (all that's left of Algar) drifts in the light.
- A **deep cold** rises from the seam, far colder than the room.
- Shoving the throne aside, or **Investigation DC 13**, opens the stair.

---

# 2. Laeren, the Keeper of the Vault

The party can find **Laeren** in the **Undercroft** beneath the Dread Market — the last hereditary **Keeper of the Vault**, an old man who has served the royal house his whole life. He hides because he knows what's down there, and Vecna's people kill what they can't use.

> **Laeren only knows the Doomed Forgotten Realms.** To him there has only ever been one Algar: the cold, lichdom-seeking **King Algar** he has served and feared for years. He has no concept of "the world before," of other universes, or of a *good* Algar — that idea simply does not exist for him. **Akilles wears the king's exact face, and Laeren cannot tell them apart.**
> *Lore reminder for you:* Akilles **is** Algar — the version from the regular Realms who killed their father, King Aldar, to stop his lichdom. The Algar of this world is the version who followed Aldar into it. Same man, same blood, two universes. Laeren has no way to know or imagine this.

Because Akilles wears the tyrant's face, showing it is dangerous — Laeren will see his king and react with terror, not hope. Two ways to play it:

- **Path A — Keep the face hidden (recommended).** Disguise Akilles (Crown of Lies, a deep hood, or just keep him back) and approach Laeren as outsiders who mean to *end* King Algar for good. Worn down by years under a monster, Laeren can be persuaded to help — even though it terrifies him. *(Keep Akilles hidden throughout; Laeren would be horrified to learn one of them shares the king's blood.)*
- **Path B — Wear the fear.** Let Laeren believe the king himself (or his agent) stands before him, and lean on that terror. Darkly ironic: the Keeper helps "the king" reach the very jar that will end him, never understanding. He complies out of fear — but a careless question ("how do I destroy my own phylactery?") will frighten or confuse him, so demands must be managed.

*An old man sits with his back to a dead shrine, wrapped in a moth-eaten Keeper's stole, sharpening a knife he is far too frail to use. When strangers step in, he tenses — a lifetime of fearing whoever comes down here. "...The vault's sealed. There's nothing for you. Please. I'm only the Keeper."*

**(If Akilles's face is seen)** *The knife clatters to the floor. The old man presses himself back against the dead shrine, shaking. "M-my king— I've kept it. I've kept everything, just as you— please, please don't—" He cannot conceive that you are anyone but the man whose vault this is.*

**What he gives (if the party earns it):**

- The phylactery is hidden among the urns in the Gallery, and **only the royal blood** can find it — a drop of it makes the true urn cry out. *(That blood is Algar's own — which Akilles carries.)*
- The urn can't be broken until the **soul-anchor below** (the Choir) is destroyed.
- To destroy the anchor, the **twelve dead kings** of the Choir must each be **named and released**, and only the **royal Castor blood** can speak the release. **Laeren knows all twelve names and the words**, and can teach them.
- **The vault smothers magic.** "Go down as men, not as wizards."

**Getting it out of him.** On **Path A**, reach him with **roleplay** — the promise to end the king, plain mercy to a frightened old man — or **spend a secret** (Power of Secrets) or roll **Persuasion DC 16** softened by good RP. On **Path B**, careful kingly demands work. Cruelty makes him clam up either way.

> **Akilles's blood works no matter what** — disguised or not, it's the royal Castor blood (Algar's own), and that's what the vault answers to. The Crown of Lies changes his appearance, not his blood.

> **Laeren's fate is the party's:** leave him in the Undercroft, get him to safety in Sigil (a recurring ally), or — if abandoned — find him dead later. He does **not** enter the vault.

---

# 3. The Hall of the Ended Kings

The entry hall below the stair. Dozens of stone effigies of kings line both walls, each carved kneeling, each holding its own crown out in offering. Every carved face has been chiseled blank.

**Who are these kings?** Simple version: for as long as this doomed Sword Coast has existed, it has been ruled by a **line of kings who each swore themselves to Vecna** for power and a promise of immortality. When each one died, the cult **stored his death here**, beneath the throne — and those hoarded deaths are what let the *current* king cheat death and reform. The urns ahead are that long line of spent kings. **Algar is just the latest.**

Flavor to sprinkle:

- The blanked faces still carry **regnal marks** — and, carved at each base, a **name** (these are the names the party will need below; **History/Investigation DC 15** to read the worn script, or just get them from Laeren).
- The air is **bone-cold and dead-quiet**.
- An inscription repeats above the effigies: **ENDED, BUT NOT RELEASED.**

---

# 4. The Gallery of Urns

*The hall opens into a vast round chamber, tiered like an amphitheater turned inward. On every plinth, on every tier, stands an urn: bonewhite, lidless, identical. Hundreds of them, climbing into dark you can't see the top of.*

The true phylactery is **one urn among hundreds**, and there is **no way to tell them apart by sight** — even *Detect Magic* shows the whole room glowing the same faint, uniform necromancy.

**Finding it — by sound, not sight (Akilles's blood).**

- When **Akilles sheds a drop of his blood**, one urn — somewhere in the gallery — begins to **cry**: a faint, continuous weeping sound (his own voice, if you want to twist the knife). **Nothing about any urn changes visibly.** The party locates the true urn **by ear.**
- The crying **fades if he stops bleeding** — he must keep a fresh drop flowing to keep the sound going while they cross the tiers toward it (a few careful minutes; **Perception** to pinpoint it in the echoing space).

*(For Akilles's personal haunting as he bleeds, see "The Pursuer" below.)*

---

# 5. The Choir of Ended Kings (the soul-anchor)

Reaching the true urn isn't enough — **it cannot be broken** while the anchor holds (see the next section for exactly what happens if they try). A short passage from the Gallery descends to the Choir.

*A round vault, domed in black stone. Twelve stone sarcophagi ring the edge — the twelve greatest of the dead kings. From each, a cord of braided bone and grey sinew runs inward to a thing hanging in the center of the room: a heart of black obsidian, fist-sized, floating, beating once every few seconds. With every beat, the twelve sarcophagi hum a single low note together — a choir with no breath.*

**What this is, plainly.** The obsidian heart is the **soul-anchor**. It feeds on the hoarded deaths of the twelve kings, and as long as it beats, Algar's phylactery upstairs **cannot be damaged**. To destroy it, the party must **release all twelve kings**, one at a time.

**The word of release (here's the actual hook).** These twelve are the ancestors of Algar's own royal line, and the vault answers to that blood — which **Akilles carries** (he *is* Algar, of the same blood). So Akilles — and only Akilles — can dismiss them. To silence a sarcophagus, speak that king's **name** and the **Castor word of release**:

> *"[Name], your oath is fulfilled. Your watch is ended. By Castor blood, be released."*

- Spoken by **Akilles** (or by someone he openly empowers to speak in his name), the sarcophagus goes silent and its cord withers.
- Spoken by anyone **without** the blood, nothing happens.
- The **twelve names** come from **Laeren** (he'll recite them) or the **effigies** in the Hall above.

### The Twelve Ended Kings (their names, if asked)

The dynasty of House Castor — the Vecna-sworn kings who ruled the doomed Sword Coast before Aldar and Algar. Oldest to most recent. The party only needs to speak the **name** (the epithet is just flavor):

1. **Castor the First-Sworn** — founder of the line; struck the original pact with Vecna.
2. **King Maladar Castor**, the Hollow Crown.
3. **King Theleron Castor**, the Whisper-King.
4. **Queen Sereval Castor**, the Ash-Veiled.
5. **King Vorhaal Castor**, the Gravebound.
6. **King Eddric Castor**, the Pale.
7. **King Sasthul Castor**, the Unmourned.
8. **King Yvorren Castor**, the Cold Lantern.
9. **Queen Halveth Castor**, the Silent.
10. **King Cazrin Castor**, the Twice-Buried.
11. **King Drevan Castor**, the Last Honest *(ironic — he was not)*.
12. **King Morwain Castor**, the Near-King — the most recent of the line laid to rest here.

*(Aldar and Algar are deliberately not among the twelve — Aldar persists as a lich elsewhere in the DFR, and Algar is the phylactery upstairs.)*

**When all twelve are silent,** the obsidian heart's beat stutters and stops; it crumbles to grit. The soul-anchor is destroyed — and the urn upstairs can now be broken.

> **The hard way (optional, with a cost).** A cord can also be physically cut (**Strength DC 14**). But a king released without his name *screams*: a necrotic pulse fills the room (**DC 15 Constitution save**, 4d8 necrotic on a fail — and no healing magic works down here). Do this twice and the noise **wakes a guardian** (the Sentinel; see stat block). The clean way is names + the word of release.

---

## The Silence of Kings (why their magic fails)

The vault hoards power the way Vecna hoards secrets, so the party's magic is choked. This is the chapter's real challenge — it pulls an overpowered party down to solving things as people.

- **Throughout the vault:** any spell of **4th level or higher** requires a **DC 18 spellcasting-ability check** or it fizzles and the slot is wasted. Concentration saves are at **disadvantage**.
- **In the Choir chamber:** a full **Antimagic Field** (as the spell). No spells, no magic-item effects, no divine intervention — they silence the Choir by voice and authority, not power.
- Describe it as spells going **grey and sputtering out**, not exploding.

---

## What Happens If They Try to Smash the Urn Early

Plainly, so you can rule it fast:

- **While the soul-anchor is intact, the urn cannot be damaged.** Attacks and spells that hit it deal **no damage** — they land like striking solid bedrock. Any fragment somehow chipped off **reattaches within a few seconds**.
- If the party keeps forcing it, the urn **drinks the assault**: each round they attack it, the reform clock **drops by 1 day** (the jar feeds on the damage), and the cold floods the room — **one guardian (the Sentinel) wakes.**
- **Bottom line for the party:** there is no brute-forcing this. Go silence the Choir first.

# The Unmaking & The Collapse

With the Choir silent and the anchor destroyed, the party returns to the crying urn. Now it breaks.

**Let Akilles do it** — the true heir ending the false king.

*You set your hand to the urn and it's only cold clay now. From inside, the crying stops. There's a single, soft sound — like a breath finally let go — and then nothing. It's over.*

**Then the vault begins to die.** With its anchor gone and its kings released, the place comes apart.

> ### 🏃 Escape (skill challenge, not a fight)
> Run the way out as a quick **group skill challenge — 4 successes before 3 failures**, each player narrating their move:
> - **Athletics / Acrobatics (DC 14)** — collapsing plinth-bridges and the buckling stair.
> - **Survival / Investigation (DC 13)** — the way up no longer matches the way down.
> - **Strength (DC 15)** — hauling a companion (or Laeren) clear of a cave-in.
> - **Magic works again** the moment they clear the Choir's dead field — let them feel their power return (movement spells, *Fly*, etc. count as successes).
>
> **On 3 failures:** no deaths — a character is briefly buried and hauled out battered. The point is breathlessness, not a TPK.

*You come up out of the stair into the empty throne room as the floor seals itself behind you. The grey dust has finally settled. Algar's throne is gone — and so is the man who sat on it.*

# The Pursuer (Akilles)

> **Context:** Jimothy already lifted Akilles's **Unseen Pursuer** bane — he's been free of it. But the phylactery *is* the doppelganger's soul, so in this vault, as Akilles bleeds, the thing he thought was gone reaches for him one last time. This is a **vault-only** echo, not the restored bane.

- While Akilles bleeds for the ritual, a figure stands at the edge of his sight — wearing **his own face**, crowned, cold — always exactly one tier behind him, never closer.
- Each time he sheds blood, **DC 16 Wisdom save** or *shaken* (−1d6 to attacks/saves) for 1 minute, hearing his own voice: *"You can't kill me. I'm the part of you that wanted the throne."*
- **The reveal:** that pursuer was always the reach of his doppelganger's soul. Jimothy treated the symptom; the source is **here**, and breaking the urn ends it forever.

# Rewards

No card draw this session, and no level-up — but the party doesn't leave empty-handed.

**① The Source Destroyed (Akilles).** Jimothy already lifted the Unseen Pursuer bane, but here the doppelganger's soul *itself* is ended — so the haunting can never return, because its source is gone. Closure on Akilles's longest-running thread, and a hard-won peace.

**② The Gratitude of the Ended Kings (party boon).** The twelve kings, *released* rather than spent, leave one shared grace: **the Quiet of Kings** — once, any party member may (as a reaction) auto-succeed on a single saving throw as the dead steady them.

**③ The Castor Signet (ring, requires attunement).** Recovered from the true urn's plinth — the signet of Akilles's true line. While worn: advantage on saves vs. *frightened*, and **3-round resistance to necrotic damage** (when activated). To one of Castor blood (Akilles), once per long rest: speak a **word of release** to end one effect compelling or binding an undead (a hostile bound undead turns neutral for 1 minute).

# Stat Block — the lone guardian

> Only used if the party brute-forces the Choir or the urn (see those sections). This is **not** an ambush; it's a consequence. One Sentinel, maybe a pair of wights, moving to stop interference — they sink back to stillness if the party disengages and works the puzzle properly. Remember the Choir is antimagic: steel and grit only.

**Sentinel of the Ended Kings**
*Medium undead, lawful evil — a crowned husk in corroded regalia.*
**AC** 17 · **HP** 95 (10d8+50) · **Speed** 30 ft.

| STR | DEX | CON | INT | WIS | CHA |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 18 (+4) | 9 (−1) | 20 (+5) | 11 (+0) | 14 (+2) | 16 (+3) |

**Saves** Con +9, Wis +6 · **Resistances** necrotic; nonmagical B/P/S · **Immunities** poison; charmed, exhaustion, frightened, poisoned · **Senses** darkvision 60 ft. · **CR** 7

- **Hoarded Stillness.** Advantage on saves vs. forced movement and prone; can't be turned. In the antimagic Choir chamber it ignores anything that needs magic to function.
- **Crown of Silence.** The first time a creature casts (or tries to cast) a spell within 30 ft., it takes 10 (3d6) psychic damage.

**Actions**
- **Multiattack.** Two Regnal Blade attacks.
- **Regnal Blade.** *+7 to hit, reach 5 ft.* — 9 (1d10+4) slashing + 7 (2d6) necrotic.
- **Sentence of the Throne (recharge 5–6).** One creature that disturbed the vault makes a **DC 15 Wisdom save** or is *paralyzed* until the end of its next turn.
