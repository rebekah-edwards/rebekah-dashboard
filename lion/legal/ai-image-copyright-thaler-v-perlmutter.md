# Thaler v. Perlmutter (AI-Generated Image Copyright) — Chronology + Source Pack

## TL;DR (for sharing)
- The **Supreme Court declined to hear** Thaler’s petition challenging the refusal to register copyright in an image generated entirely by AI.
- **No new Supreme Court rule** was issued; the denial simply leaves the lower-court outcome in place.
- The key through-line in the lower courts: **U.S. copyright registration requires human authorship** (at least where no human author is identified).

---

## Chronology (story first, then links)

### 1) District Court (D.D.C.) — refusal affirmed (human authorship required)
- **Event:** U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia upheld the Copyright Office’s refusal to register an AI-generated image where no human author was identified.
- **Primary source (PDF):** District Court decision (hosted by U.S. Copyright Office)
  - https://www.copyright.gov/ai/docs/district-court-decision-affirming-refusal-of-registration.pdf
- **Docket (free mirror):** CourtListener docket for the district-court case (1:22-cv-01564)
  - https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/63356475/thaler-v-perlmutter/

### 2) Court of Appeals (D.C. Circuit) — affirmance
- **Event:** U.S. Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit affirmed, addressing the “human authorship” requirement in this posture (where no human author was identified).
- **Primary source (PDF):** D.C. Circuit opinion (No. 23-5233)
  - https://media.cadc.uscourts.gov/opinions/docs/2025/03/23-5233.pdf
- **Docket (free mirror):** CourtListener docket for the D.C. Circuit appeal (23-5233)
  - https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/67892225/stephen-thaler-v-shira-perlmutter/

### 3) Supreme Court — cert denied
- **Event:** The Supreme Court denied Thaler’s petition for certiorari (declined to hear the case).
- **Primary source (PDF):** Supreme Court order list referenced by IPWatchdog
  - https://www.supremecourt.gov/orders/courtorders/030226zor_2d8f.pdf

## Notes / guardrails for sharing publicly (draft)
- A denial of cert **does not equal** Supreme Court endorsement of every line in the lower opinions.
- The big unanswered question remains: **how much AI contribution is compatible with “human authorship”** when a human uses AI as a tool.
