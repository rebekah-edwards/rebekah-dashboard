# The Surge of CRT in Literature — Support Pack

> Goal: support a social post / blog / video arguing that **critical race theory and adjacent race-conscious frameworks are visibly present in literature teaching, publishing discourse, and current censorship battles**, without overclaiming that "all literature is now CRT."

## Recommendation
Use this as a **measured, evidence-led industry commentary** piece.

Best-supported framing:
1. **CRT is not confined to law schools**; it appears explicitly in literature pedagogy.
2. **Publishing institutions have spent years measuring themselves through diversity/equity frameworks**, which shows race-conscious institutional framing is mainstream.
3. **The fiercest fights over books are disproportionately about race, identity, and power**, which shows these questions are central to how literature is contested in public life.

## Core thesis
**The debate over CRT in literature is not imaginary. You can see it in how literature is taught, how publishing institutions talk about representation and equity, and which books are most likely to be challenged or banned.**

Short version:
**CRT in literature is less a single smoking gun than an institutional pattern.**

---

## A) Literature pedagogy: CRT is explicitly present

### A1) MLA volume includes CRT as a literature-teaching method
- **Claim:** The Modern Language Association includes CRT explicitly inside literature pedagogy.
  - Source: MLA — *Teaching Law and Literature*
    - https://www.mla.org/Publications/Bookstore/Options-for-Teaching/Teaching-Law-and-Literature
    - Relevant contents listing includes: **"Using Critical Race Theory to Teach African American Literature"**

**Useable line:**
> CRT is not only a legal theory; it has entered the teaching of literature itself.

### A2) MLA teaching materials also normalize CRT as one framework among many in literary study
- **Claim:** MLA materials describe CRT as one of the theoretical approaches available to literature instructors.
  - Source: MLA — *Teaching Central American Literature in a Global Context*
    - https://www.mla.org/Publications/Bookstore/Options-for-Teaching/Teaching-Central-American-Literature-in-a-Global-Context
    - Description references theoretical approaches including **critical race theory**.

**Useable line:**
> In mainstream literature-teaching infrastructure, CRT is presented as a legitimate interpretive framework, not as an exotic fringe idea.

---

## B) Publishing institutions: race-conscious framing is mainstream

### B1) Lee & Low / PW diversity baseline numbers
- **Claim:** The publishing workforce remains heavily white, but the industry has spent years publicly measuring itself through diversity/equity categories.
  - Source: *Publishers Weekly* (Feb. 28, 2024) — *New Lee & Low Diversity Baseline Survey Finds Minor Changes*
    - https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/publisher-news/article/94466-new-lee-low-diversity-baseline-survey-finds-minor-changes.html
    - Key figures reported from the 2023 survey:
      - **72.5% white** in 2023
      - down from **76%** in 2019
      - down from **79%** in 2015

- **Claim:** The same report frames the post-2020 environment in explicitly DEI terms.
  - Source: same PW article above
    - Notes that the 2023 survey followed the murder of George Floyd in 2020 and a renewed industry commitment to diversity, equity, and inclusion.

**Useable line:**
> Whatever label people prefer — diversity, equity, anti-racism, systemic critique — race-conscious institutional framing is now normal in publishing.

---

## C) Public conflict over books: race/identity content is disproportionately targeted

### C1) PEN America: book bans remain massive in scale
- **Claim:** The 2023–2024 school year saw a very high volume of book bans.
  - Source: PEN America — *Banned in the USA: Beyond the Shelves*
    - https://pen.org/report/beyond-the-shelves/
    - PEN reports **10,046 instances of book bans** across **29 states** and **220 public school districts** in the 2023–2024 school year.

### C2) PEN America: race/identity-linked titles are heavily targeted
- **Claim:** Banned-title patterns are strongly concentrated around race and identity.
  - Source: PEN America press release (Mar. 14, 2025)
    - https://pen.org/press-release/book-bans-in-schools-sweep-across-reading-levels-genres-and-topics-while-censorship-erases-stories-about-people-of-color-and-lgbtq-topics-most-often/
    - Among **4,218 unique banned titles** in 2023–2024:
      - **36%** featured fictional characters or real people of color
      - **29%** included LGBTQ+ characters, people, or themes
      - **20%** involved race and racism
      - **15%** involved activism and social movements
      - **13%** touched on incarceration and policing

**Useable line:**
> Even if people fight over the label “CRT,” the real-world battle over literature is clearly centered on race, identity, and power.

---

## D) Best-supported post framing

### What we can argue confidently
- CRT and related race-conscious frameworks are **explicitly present in literature pedagogy**.
- Publishing institutions now operate in a world where **representation/equity language is routine and measurable**.
- The public culture-war battle over books is **heavily concentrated on race, identity, sexuality, and power**.

### What we should avoid claiming
Avoid unless we build a stronger dataset:
- "Most literature is now CRT"
- "Universities universally teach literature through CRT"
- "Mainstream fiction has been fully taken over"
- "There was a sudden surge" if we cannot date and quantify the surge specifically

Safer phrasing:
- **increasingly visible**
- **institutionally present**
- **embedded in some teaching frameworks**
- **part of the broader publishing and education discourse**
- **central to current cultural conflict over books**
